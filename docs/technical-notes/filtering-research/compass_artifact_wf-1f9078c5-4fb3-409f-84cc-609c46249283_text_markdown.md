# Deterministic Pre-Filtering and Triage of Web Pages Before LLM Extraction: Design Patterns for Detecting School Bell Schedules

## TL;DR
- Build a **layered, deterministic pre-filter modeled on Snorkel-style labeling functions**: many cheap, independent boolean detectors (schema.org parse, `<address>`/vCard class match, time-range regex, footer-proximity heuristics) whose votes are combined by a transparent weighted/majority scheme into three tiers — high-confidence YES, high-confidence NO, and an ambiguous middle that is the only tier sent to an LLM. Published cascades (FrugalGPT, video moderation, job classification) show this routes only a minority of documents to the expensive model; FrugalGPT reports overall cost savings ranging "from 50% to 98%" while matching GPT-4-level quality.
- The **schema.org "cheap win" is real but low-yield for schools**: OpeningHoursSpecification/openingHours markup is common on the web generally (LocalBusiness annotation grew from 386K to 1.2M domains, 310% growth over 2018–2022 per Web Data Commons), but a targeted investigation found **no evidence that the major K-12 CMS platforms (Finalsite, Apptegy, Edlio, Blackboard WCM, Foxbright, SchoolMessenger) auto-generate hours markup**, and even when present it typically encodes office/building hours, not instructional bell times. Build the parser first anyway — it is ~20 lines with `extruct` and near-zero false positives — but treat it as one weak signal, not the primary detector.
- For the footer/sidebar "Hours" block problem, **do not rely on readability-style single-block extractors**, which discard exactly those zones as boilerplate. Use trafilatura with `favor_recall=True` and `include_tables=True`, or operate directly on the DOM with targeted heuristics (short `<ul>` near `<footer>`, `<address>` tags, `class`/`id` tokens matching `hours|contact|vcard|adr`). Calibrate the deterministic-stage threshold against a hand-labeled set to a fixed **recall target (e.g., ≥95–99%)** using a SUPG-style sampling method so the cheap filter provably does not silently discard true positives.

## Key Findings

**1. Weak supervision / labeling-function architectures are the right mental model.** Snorkel (Ratner et al., 2017) formalizes combining many noisy heuristics: each *labeling function* (LF) consumes a data point and emits a class vote or *abstains* (returns -1). A *generative label model* learns each LF's accuracy and correlations without ground truth and produces probabilistic labels; it provably beats unweighted majority vote when LF accuracies are heterogeneous, but converges to majority vote at very low or very high label density. Snorkel DryBell (Bach et al., SIGMOD 2019) deployed this at Google on content-classification tasks, creating "classifiers of comparable quality to ones trained with tens of thousands of hand-labeled examples" (specifically matching models trained on 12,000 and 80,000 hand-labeled points) and yielding "an average 52% performance improvement" from non-servable organizational-knowledge LFs. Key LF diagnostics — polarity, coverage, overlap, conflict — are directly reusable to audit our detectors.

**2. schema.org hours markup: a cheap shortcut with important limits.** OpeningHoursSpecification and the older `openingHours` property are well-defined and machine-parseable (schema.org's LocalBusiness example encodes it as `"openingHours": ["Mo-Fr 09:00-17:00"]`). Web Data Commons (extracting schema.org from Common Crawl since 2013; Brinkmann, Primpeli & Bizer, WWW2023) reports LocalBusiness annotation grew "from 386K to 1.2M (310% growth)" over 2018–2022, with overall schema.org adoption reaching 37.9% of all pay-level domains in the crawl by 2022. Crucially, a subagent investigation corrected a common misconception: `School` and `EducationalOrganization` have **dual inheritance** (Thing > Place > CivicStructure > EducationalOrganization AND Thing > Organization > EducationalOrganization), so they DO inherit `openingHours`/`openingHoursSpecification` from CivicStructure/Place — hours markup on a school entity is vocabulary-legal. The real problems: (a) adoption on school CMSs appears near-zero; (b) the property semantically encodes building/office access hours, not bell schedules; (c) Google documents no rich result for school hours, removing the SEO incentive to add it.

**3. Boilerplate removal discards what we want; use recall-favoring or DOM-direct extraction.** Readability, Boilerpipe/DOM Distiller, Dragnet, Web2Text, and BoilerNet are all built to extract ONE main content block and discard nav/footer/sidebar. Boilerpipe's own analysis found footers/sidebars are dropped via link-density and word-count features. trafilatura exposes the escape hatches: `favor_recall=True` ("when unsure, prefer more text"), `include_tables=True` (default), `include_links`, and `prune_xpath`. None of these tools ship a reusable "this is a business-hours block" detector, so that heuristic must be built.

**4. Two-stage triage with a calibrated recall floor is a documented production pattern.** FrugalGPT (Chen, Zaharia & Zou, arXiv:2305.05176 / TMLR 2024) cascades cheap→expensive models, with overall cost savings ranging "from 50% to 98%" while matching GPT-4's downstream performance. Industrial video-moderation and job-classification pipelines use a cheap recall stage then an expensive ranker/LLM. The critical methodology is threshold calibration: cascade literature (e.g., Viola-Jones) sets each cheap stage's threshold to a very high recall (99.x%) so the compounded recall stays high; SUPG (Kang et al., PVLDB 2020) provides statistical machinery to pick a proxy-score cutoff that meets a user-specified recall target (e.g., "90% recall and a failure probability of at most 5%") via importance-weighted oracle sampling.

## Details

### Area 1 — Weak-supervision / "labeling function" architectures

**The core pattern.** Instead of training one categorical classifier, express each cheap signal as an independent boolean/vote function. In Snorkel's formulation (Ratner et al., 2017, *Snorkel: Rapid Training Data Creation with Weak Supervision*, VLDB Journal) each LF returns a class label or abstains (`-1`). The system models the true label as a latent variable and fits a *generative model* over LF votes, "a noisy 'voter' which is independent," optionally modeling dependencies to avoid the "double counting" problem when two LFs encode similar heuristics.

**How votes are combined.** Three escalating options, all applicable without training:
- **Majority / weighted majority vote.** Snorkel's `MajorityLabelVoter` is the simplest; ties can `abstain`, break randomly, or by deterministic hash. Practically, teams often find a hand-weighted vote competitive (Snorkel GitHub issue #1073).
- **Generative/label model.** Snorkel's `LabelModel.fit(L)` learns LF weights from the agreement/disagreement structure of the label matrix alone (no gold labels). It "down-weights low-accuracy LFs and amplifies high-accuracy ones," outperforming majority vote when accuracies are heterogeneous. Per the Snorkel paper and a Towards Data Science synthesis, the generative model helps most at *medium label density* and matches majority vote at low/high density.
- **Conflict resolution.** LF dependencies are modeled explicitly; DryBell notes that if dependencies "are not properly handled, the estimated labels could be biased or systematically wrong."

**Diagnostics to reuse directly.** Snorkel's `LFAnalysis` computes per-LF **polarity** (labels emitted), **coverage** (fraction labeled), **overlap** (≥2 LFs fire), and **conflicts** (LFs disagree). For our task these become a live dashboard: e.g., "the JSON-LD LF has 4% coverage but 99% accuracy; the footer-regex LF has 60% coverage but conflicts 15% of the time."

**Real-world web-content precedents.** Snorkel DryBell (arXiv:1812.00417) deployed weak supervision on "content and real-time event classification applications at Google," achieving "an average 52% performance improvement" from organizational-knowledge LFs and executing over millions of points in tens of minutes. Other write-ups: weakly supervised bank-transaction classification (arXiv:2305.18430) and weak supervision for search-system precision (arXiv:2503.07025). The takeaway for our system: implement each detector as an LF returning `{YES:1, NO:0, ABSTAIN:-1}`, log the label matrix, and start with weighted majority vote — only graduate to a learned label model if diagnostics show heterogeneous accuracies and medium label density.

**Concrete LFs for bell-schedule detection:**
- `lf_jsonld_hours`: parse with `extruct`; return YES if any OpeningHoursSpecification/openingHours found, else ABSTAIN.
- `lf_time_range_regex`: match `\b(1[0-2]|0?[1-9])(:[0-5]\d)?\s*([AaPp]\.?[Mm]\.?)\s*[-–—to]+\s*...` for a start–end pair; return YES if ≥1 pair in a short block, else ABSTAIN.
- `lf_address_tag`: YES if `<address>` present or element `class`/`id` contains `vcard|adr|hours|contact`.
- `lf_footer_list`: YES if a short `<ul>` (≤6 items) sits within/near `<footer>` and contains a time pattern.
- `lf_bell_keyword`: YES if page text or URL slug contains `bell schedule|start time|dismissal|daily schedule`.
- `lf_dominant_type_penalty`: NO/abstain shaping if the page is dominated by a news feed / calendar / agenda pattern (many dated list items, no isolated time-range block).

### Area 2 — schema.org / microdata detection for hours

**What to detect.** Two vocabularies: the flat `openingHours` Text property (`"Mo-Fr 08:00-15:00"`, hyphenated day/time ranges, 24h) and the richer `OpeningHoursSpecification` object (`dayOfWeek`, `opens`, `closes`, `validFrom/validThrough`). Three encodings: JSON-LD (`<script type="application/ld+json">`, Google-recommended), Microdata (`itemprop="openingHours"`), and RDFa.

**How to parse it (deterministic, no ML).** Use `extruct` (scrapinghub) — one call extracts JSON-LD, Microdata, RDFa, OpenGraph, Microformat, Dublin Core and can `uniform=True` the output. trafilatura also parses JSON-LD internally (`extract_meta_json` walks `//script[@type="application/ld+json"]`). A minimal detector: `extruct.extract(html, syntaxes=["json-ld","microdata"])`, then recursively search for keys `openingHours`/`openingHoursSpecification`.

**Prevalence reality.** Over 45M domains use some schema.org markup (schema.org, 2024); schema:Organization adoption alone grew "1.3M PLDs to 2.2M PLDs from 2018 to 2019" (Web Data Commons), and LocalBusiness grew 386K→1.2M domains 2013–2022. BUT the subagent found: (a) **no documentation or third-party report that Finalsite, Apptegy/Thrillshare, Edlio, Blackboard WCM (now Anthology/Finalsite), Foxbright, SharpSchool, or SchoolMessenger Presence auto-generate hours markup** — their SEO guidance centers on keywords, meta tags, headings, and ADA compliance, never JSON-LD; (b) EducationalOrganization/School is rare enough that Web Data Commons does not track it as a notable class; (c) bell schedules are rendered as a heterogeneous mix of HTML tables/lists, embedded PDFs, and images, none carrying schema.org time annotations.

**Verdict on the "cheap win."** Build the schema.org LF first — it is trivial (~20 lines), runs in single-digit milliseconds, and has near-zero false-positive rate — so any page that DOES carry hours markup is resolved for free. But calibrate expectations: coverage on school pages will be low, and a positive hit likely encodes office hours, so pair it with a differentiator (bell-schedule keyword context, `EducationalOrganization`/`School` `@type`, or presence of multiple period rows) before routing to YES for *instructional* times specifically.

### Area 3 — DOM-structure-aware extraction preserving multiple informative zones

**Why standard tools fail here.** Boilerplate removal is defined as keeping one main block. Kohlschütter, Fankhauser & Nejdl (2010, *Boilerplate Detection Using Shallow Text Features*, WSDM '10, pp. 441–450) show that combining word count "and link density leads to a simple classification model that achieves competitive accuracy" (they rank "number of words quotient, text density quotient, number of words, and the link and text densities" as the highest-information-gain features); footers and contact blocks, being short and link-heavy, are exactly what gets dropped. Boilerpipe (and Chrome's DOM Distiller, which uses it) and Readability.js apply this logic. Dragnet ("just the facts," WWW2013) and the neural successors Web2Text (ECIR 2018, CNN over DOM features + Viterbi, F1 0.96 on Dragnet data) and BoilerNet also target a single main-content labeling. Notably, SemText (2021) improved on BoilerNet specifically by adding class info (e.g., "sidebar", "question list") to reduce mislabeling — evidence that CSS-class signals carry the discriminative power we need.

**trafilatura configuration for our case.** Documented options (readthedocs, 2.1.0): `favor_recall=True` ("when unsure, prefer more text"), `favor_precision=True` (opposite), `include_tables=True` (default — captures `<table>` bell schedules), `include_links=True`, `include_comments`, and `prune_xpath` to drop known-junk subtrees before extraction. For maximizing capture of a stray footer "Hours" line, favor recall and keep tables/links. trafilatura is heuristic (no ML/GPU), single-digit ms/page, with a readability-lxml + jusText fallback chain.

**No tool ships a "business-hours block" detector — build it.** Reusable deterministic heuristics, grounded in real conventions:
- **Semantic HTML:** the `<address>` element. Microformats guidance: "Every `<address>` on a page SHOULD be an hCard," and hCard's root marker is `class="vcard"` (or `h-card`), parseable by the CSS selector `.vcard`.
- **Microformat classes (hCard/vCard):** look for `vcard`/`h-card` roots and property classes `adr`, `street-address`, `locality`, `tel`, `org`, `fn`. These are stable, case-sensitive, lowercase tokens (microformats.org). An org's hCard uses `fn org` on one element.
- **CSS class/id token match:** regex the `class`/`id` attributes for `hours|open|schedule|contact|footer|vcard|adr|info`.
- **Structural proximity:** short `<ul>` (≤~6 `<li>`) or short `<div>`/`<p>` within or adjacent to `<footer>`, containing a time-range regex match, low link density, and few total words — the inverse of the Kohlschütter "main content" profile.
- **Time-pattern regex** as the payload confirmer (see Area 1). Rule-based time taggers (SUTime, HeidelTime) exist but a targeted regex is faster and sufficient for `H:MM AM–H:MM PM` / 24h ranges.

Operate on the parsed DOM (lxml / BeautifulSoup) so you can score *every* candidate zone independently rather than accepting one winner — this is the crucial architectural inversion versus readability.

### Area 4 — Two-stage triage pipeline best practices

**The pattern.** A cheap deterministic scorer partitions documents into accept (YES) / reject (NO) / defer (send to LLM). Only the deferred middle band incurs model cost. This is the model-cascade / "cheap filter then expensive model" pattern: FrugalGPT (arXiv:2305.05176) "can match the performance of the best individual LLM (e.g. GPT-4) with up to 98% cost reduction"; a Gemini-probes paper (arXiv:2601.11516) describes a "heuristic band method" deferring queries whose probe logit falls in `[t−δ, t+δ]`; industrial video moderation (arXiv:2507.17204) uses a "lightweight router (recall stage)" then an MLLM ranker; a job-classifier engineering write-up (Teotti) layered "keyword filters for the obvious cases, ML for the bulk, LLM for the nuanced decisions" and raised recall from 68.74% into the 90s.

**Setting the thresholds.** Two calibration philosophies:
- **High-recall cheap stage (cascade tradition).** Viola-Jones-style cascades set each cheap stage to very high recall (~99.8% per stage) so compounded recall stays high; false positives are cleaned up downstream. Apply this to the NO tier: only auto-reject when *all* LFs abstain/vote NO and no time pattern exists anywhere, so true positives are essentially never discarded early.
- **Statistically guaranteed recall (SUPG).** Kang, Gan, Bailis, Hashimoto & Zaharia (2020), *Approximate Selection with Guarantees using Proxies* (PVLDB 13(12); arXiv:2004.00827), target "a minimum recall (RT queries)... subject to an oracle label budget and a failure probability" — e.g., "90% recall and a failure probability of at most 5%," formally Pr[Recall(R) ≥ γr] ≥ 1−δ. SUPG orders records by proxy score, then uses **importance sampling with weights proportional to the square root of proxy scores** plus a multiple-hypothesis correction to choose a cutoff τ that guarantees the recall target — improving result quality "by up to 30x" over naive uniform sampling on a validation set. Successors (GAMCAL/SUPG-IT in "Streaming Model Cascades for Semantic SQL," arXiv:2604.00660) add learned calibration and joint precision-recall targets; ScaleDoc notes SUPG can still miss targets under distribution shift, so monitor continuously.

**Measuring recall loss at the cheap stage (the safety-critical step).**
1. **Hand-label a stratified sample** of pages (including hard mixed pages: news-dominant with a footer hours line, PDF-only schedules, table schedules).
2. **Compute the confusion matrix at the chosen cutoff** and report **recall AND pass-through rate at the operating point**, plus a threshold sensitivity sweep (±0.05) — exactly the transparency the two-stage ML literature recommends (arXiv:2510.03959: "report the pass-through rate and recall at the operating point alongside AUCPR... and a brief threshold sensitivity").
3. **Track false negatives explicitly** — the pages the cheap filter sent to NO that were actually YES. This is the "silently discarding true positives" failure mode; it is invisible unless you sample the auto-rejected tier.
4. **Re-audit under drift** (new CMS templates, redesigns), since LFs "designed for one corpus may have very different accuracy on another."

**Recommended tiering logic for this task:**
- **Auto-YES (no LLM):** valid schema.org hours on a school entity WITH bell-schedule context, OR a clean single time-range block in an `<address>`/vcard/footer zone with bell keyword nearby.
- **Auto-NO (no LLM):** no time pattern anywhere in the DOM AND no schema.org hours AND no bell keyword — and only after recall validation shows this rule's false-negative rate is under the target.
- **Defer to LLM:** everything else — multiple competing time blocks, time patterns embedded in prose/news, table schedules needing structure interpretation, or schema.org office hours that may or may not be bell times.

## Recommendations

1. **Build in this order (fastest wins first):**
   (a) schema.org LF via `extruct` — cheapest, near-zero FP; (b) DOM-zone LFs (`<address>`/vcard class match, footer short-list proximity, CSS token match); (c) time-range regex payload confirmer; (d) bell-keyword and dominant-type-penalty LFs.
2. **Combine with weighted majority vote first.** Log the full label matrix and Snorkel-style diagnostics (coverage, overlap, conflict). Only adopt a learned `LabelModel` if diagnostics show heterogeneous LF accuracy at medium label density — otherwise it won't beat the transparent vote.
3. **Extract with recall favored.** Use `trafilatura.extract(html, favor_recall=True, include_tables=True, include_links=True)` for the text channel, but ALSO run direct DOM scoring so non-adjacent zones each get an independent vote.
4. **Calibrate the NO tier to a fixed recall floor.** Choose a target (start at **≥98% recall** given the high value of each true positive), and set the cutoff with a SUPG-style importance-sampled procedure against a hand-labeled set rather than eyeballing a validation split. Report recall + pass-through + a ±0.05 sensitivity sweep.
5. **Instrument the auto-rejected tier.** Periodically hand-label a sample of Auto-NO pages to measure the false-negative rate directly; this is the only way to catch silent recall loss.
6. **Escalate only the middle.** Send the deferred band to the LLM; feed it the recall-favored extracted text PLUS the specific candidate zones the DOM heuristics flagged, to keep prompts short and cheap.

**Thresholds that change the plan:** If auditing shows the schema.org LF coverage on your school corpus is effectively 0%, deprioritize it (still keep it — it's cheap). If the deferred (LLM) tier exceeds ~30–40% of documents, your deterministic LFs are too timid — tighten the Auto-YES/Auto-NO rules using conflict analysis. If false-negative rate in the Auto-NO tier exceeds your target, widen the defer band (shrink Auto-NO) until recall is restored.

## Caveats
- **Negative findings on CMS schema.org generation are documentation-level, not from a live-source crawl.** The subagent confirmed no platform *advertises or documents* auto-generated hours markup, but did not grep a statistical sample of live school HTML. Confirm with a Screaming Frog / custom crawl before fully deprioritizing the schema.org path.
- **The dual-inheritance correction matters:** contrary to a common assumption, `School`/`EducationalOrganization` DO inherit `openingHours`, so absence of hours markup is an adoption gap, not a vocabulary limitation.
- **schema.org hours ≠ bell times.** Even a valid hit usually means office/building hours; always apply a differentiator before treating it as instructional start/end.
- **Boilerplate benchmarks are genre-dependent.** Reported F1 numbers (e.g., Web2Text 0.96 on Dragnet) come from news-article corpora; performance on school CMS pages with mixed feeds is unmeasured and likely lower — validate on your own labeled set.
- **SUPG-style guarantees assume calibrated proxies and stable distributions.** Under CMS redesigns or new templates the guarantee can break (ScaleDoc); continuous monitoring and periodic recalibration are required.
- **Some content is genuinely non-deterministic to extract** (schedule-as-image, schedule-as-PDF, prose-embedded times). These *should* route to the LLM (or OCR + LLM); the goal is to minimize LLM calls, not eliminate them.
