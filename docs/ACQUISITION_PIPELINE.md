---

## Goal

Per district, **daily instructional minutes per grade band (elementary / middle / high)** — the LCT numerator — for ~20,000 U.S. districts. We optimize for **school-level bell-schedule collection** (express per-band minute statements are rare), then aggregate to a district band value.

---

## The 9-stage pipeline

```
1 Queue    → 2 Discover (waves) → 3 Capture (tiered) → 4 Local process (OCR/text)
          → 5 Local filter (coarse) → 6 Hand to OpenRouter → 7 Extract (council, per-school)
          → 8 Aggregate (modal→mean) → 9 Incorporate (DB, or statutory fallback)
```

### 1 · Queue — designed and built 2026-06-22

Builds a batch of districts (default 12) plus, per district, the per-band school lists to target — the structured input Stage 2 (Discover) and Stage 3 (Capture) consume. Source data: NCES `ccd_lea_029_2425` (district directory + claimed grade span) and `ccd_sch_029_2425` (school directory + per-school grade span), cross-referenced against the DB (`enrollment_by_grade`, `districts.is_shared_service_entity`). Code: `infrastructure/acquisition/stage1_queue/queue_batch.py`, reusing `school_sampling.py` (extended) and the new `district_status.py` registry.

**Pre-queue exclusion filters** (applied in this order to build the eligible pool; all are *live filters recomputed every run*, never a persisted exclusion list — a future policy change lets a district back in for free, no cleanup needed):

1. **Not operating** — LEA-level `SY_STATUS_TEXT != "Open"` (closed/inactive/future agencies).
2. **CTC / shared-service entity** — `districts.is_shared_service_entity = True`. Backfilled 2026-06-22 via `infrastructure/database/migrations/apply_ctc_classification.py` (600 districts; name-pattern match on career/technical/vocational/jted/cted **OR** NCES `LEA_TYPE_TEXT` in `{Specialized public school district, Service agency, State operated agency}`, disambiguated from charter LEAs via `LEA_TYPE_TEXT`). Expanded from an initial name-only pass of 152 after Pima County JTED slipped through into a live batch (acronym name, didn't literally spell "technical") — see `METHODOLOGY.md` Rule 6 for the full identification logic and the accepted trade-off (the blanket `LEA_TYPE_TEXT` buckets also catch some legitimate full-time special-purpose state schools, e.g. deaf/blind institutes, that aren't really CTCs). This exclusion was schema-only and silently a no-op in every LCT calculation until 2026-06-22.
3. **Grade-span integrity (Rule 7)** — exclude **and** flag when the LEA-level claimed span (`bands_for()` applied to the district's overall `GSLO`/`GSHI`) includes a band that the school-level union (`bands_for()` applied per-school, via `ccd_sch_029`) covers with **zero** schools. A K-8-only district legitimately has no high band (not claimed, not a gap); a K-12 district with schools spanning only K-5 and 9-12 (nothing covering 6-8) is internally inconsistent and untrustworthy to sample from. See `METHODOLOGY.md` Rule 7. **Impact: ~985 districts excluded** (run 2026-06-22, after the school-level filtering fix below — roughly doubled from an initial 485, since districts whose only "covering" school in a band was a CTC/alternative/special-ed/standalone-preschool school now correctly show that band as uncovered rather than falsely covered); **rose again to 1,439** after the `recursive_band_groups()` per-segment-overlap fix (see Stage 1 decision log) — the old position-based rule had been masking real gaps nationally, not just in the districts that motivated the fix.
4. **Already attempted** — any `district_id` that has reached **Stage 3 (Capture) or beyond** in the `district_status.json` registry (`data/acquisition/status/`, see below) is excluded, **regardless of prior outcome** (success or failure). Don't resample a district that already had a real, costly attempt; failures get triaged as a separate, deliberate process, not silently retried. **A district whose furthest recorded stage is 1 (Queue) or 2 (Discover) is NOT excluded** — those stages target/search only, with no real fetch yet, so they stay eligible for redraw (corrected 2026-06-22: excluding on *any* recorded stage silently dropped every district from the prior, never-captured `batch_00001` on re-queue, masking a Stage-1 bug fix instead of letting it be re-confirmed on the same districts). See `district_status.ATTEMPTED_THRESHOLD_STAGE`.

A district must also have non-null, non-zero `enrollment_k12` (most recent year with usable data) to participate — enrollment is the primary stratification axis (next).

**Stratified sampling** — enrollment is the priority axis, state is a secondary tiebreak (not an independent axis: staff count and school count were considered and dropped, too collinear with enrollment to buy independent coverage beyond what enrollment alone provides):

1. Sort the current eligible pool by `enrollment_k12` and split into 4 equal-**count** quartiles (not equal-value-range — NCES enrollment is heavily right-skewed, so equal-range buckets would be wildly unequal in population).
2. Target 3 districts per quartile (4 × 3 = 12) — same fixed-mix-per-stratum shape as the retired `training_batch.py` (which used 3 school-count tiers instead of enrollment quartiles).
3. Each individual pick (not just each bucket) prefers a state not yet used in this batch, seeded-shuffled otherwise — `seed = batch_id`.
4. If a quartile runs short, top up from the overall remaining pool (which is disproportionately the adjacent quartiles, since exhausted buckets contribute nothing) until the batch reaches its target size or the pool is exhausted.

**Per-band school selection** — per selected district, target up to 12 schools per band (elementary/middle/high) from the school-level NCES roster (never LEA-level — LEA-level grade span has no per-school granularity, see Rule 7 above), full census if a band has ≤12 candidates:

- **School-level filtering (added 2026-06-22, found by human review of a real batch — CP-A doing exactly its job):** `school_index()` only admits NCES `SCH_TYPE_TEXT = "Regular School"` and excludes standalone preschool/early-childhood schools (`GSHI in ("PK", "KG")`, no grade-1+). Surfaced by real schools in `batch_00001`: **Olympic Peninsula HomeConnection** (`SCH_TYPE_TEXT = "Alternative School"` — a homeschool-umbrella program, near-certain bell-schedule-collection failure), **Lake Preschool** (`GSLO=GSHI=PK`, no normal academic day to capture), **Jackson County Vocational Center** (`SCH_TYPE_TEXT = "Career and Technical School"` — a school-level CTC inside an otherwise-normal district, invisible to the LEA-level Rule 6 exclusion since the surrounding LEA isn't a CTC), and **Clinton County Early Childhood Center** (`GSLO=PK, GSHI=KG` — the `GSHI=="PK"` rule alone missed this, extended to also catch `GSHI=="KG"`). NCES's `SCH_TYPE_TEXT` is a structural field (Regular / Special Education / Career and Technical / Alternative), not a name heuristic — deliberately not name-matched, since plausible normal-school names ("Mountain Home Elementary") would collide. **Special Education School is excluded too**, accepted for now as a known, deferred gap (the project's SPED staffing work already flags this as a category needing dedicated attention later, not a clean fit for this filter's rationale).
- **LEVEL-primary classification + recursive grade-range grouping (added 2026-06-22, refined same day after a user-built reference table of recognized grade-band shapes — see decision log in `docs/diagrams/acquisition_pipeline_flow.md`):** band membership is no longer pure grade-range overlap. `school_index()` assigns a school to exactly one band immediately when NCES `LEVEL` is a clean `Elementary`/`Middle`/`High` — full stop, regardless of grade-range overlap with an adjacent band ("our primary tool is to rely on LEVEL before any of this"). For whatever LEVEL leaves unresolved (a band with zero clean candidates, or a school whose own LEVEL is ambiguous — `Other`/`Secondary`/`Not reported`/`Ungraded`/blank), `recursive_band_groups()` groups the district's distinct grade spans by structural position, *when they form a clean ascending, non-overlapping partition*: consecutive leading segments with top grade ≤6 collapse into elementary (handles 1, 2, or 3+ elementary sub-segments — lower/upper elementary, primary/intermediate splits); what remains is middle alone, middle+high merged (no separate middle identity exists at all), or middle alone followed by one-or-more high segments (lower-high/upper-high splits, e.g. a 9th-grade campus + main high school). If the spans *don't* form a clean partition (genuinely different, overlapping/redundant spans — not just multiple buildings sharing one identical span), fall back to the original any-overlap rescue, unvalidated for that messier shape. Two distinct real failure modes drove building this: **dilution** — Fayette County IN's real `Connersville Middle School` was getting diluted 6-to-1 by 5 unrelated K-6 elementary schools that merely clipped the boundary grade; **false-gap risk** — Calhan CO and Breathitt County KY have *no* school labeled "Middle" at all, so a strict LEVEL-only rule would wrongly trip Rule 7. Why not enumerate every district's grade-split convention (K-4/5-8, K-6/7-9, K-8, etc.) by hand? Because NCES has already done that per-school classification for us via `LEVEL` where it applies cleanly, and the recursive structural rule covers the rest without needing a lookup table at all.
- **Why "exactly N schools" is the wrong scoping question, found by profiling the full corpus:** an earlier version of this fix scoped itself to "exactly 2 schools" specifically to avoid touching Breathitt County KY/Chama Valley NM/Northern Tioga PA (3+ schools each). Profiling all 17,265 eligible districts against a reference table of recognized grade-band shapes showed this was the wrong dividing line. Northern Tioga's 3 elementaries share an *identical* `KG-06` span and its 2 secondaries share an *identical* `07-12` span — collapsed to distinct spans, that's the exact same 2-segment shape as Jasper Co. MO, and it now gets the same fix (previously it did not — a real correction, not a refinement). Breathitt County and Chama Valley are genuinely different: their elementary spans are *not* identical and one subsumes another (e.g. `PK-06` entirely contains `PK-02` and `03-06`) — not a clean partition, so they correctly stay on the conservative fallback. The dividing line is "does this district's spans form a clean partition," answerable directly from the data — not school count.
- **Grade-13 bug fix (found in the same review):** `bands_for()` had no entry for NCES grade code "13" (a real, sanctioned code some states use for a continuation/extra-year high school program), so any school coded e.g. `GSLO=09/GSHI=13` silently classified into **zero** bands. This wasn't a hypothetical — it surfaced because Jackson County MS's three real comprehensive high schools (East Central, St. Martin, Vancleave, all `09-13`) were invisible to band selection, leaving the Vocational Center as the *only* visible high-band candidate, which is why `band_processing_order` put "high" first (most-constrained = fewest candidates) in a way that looked wrong until traced back. Fixed by adding grade 13 to `GRADE_ORD` as part of the high band. **222 open schools nationally use `GSHI=13`** — not a one-district issue.
- **Virtual school exclusion + any-overlap fallback grade-6 dilution (both found 2026-06-22, same CP-A pass):** Virginia Connections Academy (a Pearson-operated full-virtual school, NCES `ccd_sch_129` `VIRTUAL_TEXT="Exclusively virtual"`) was being rescued into both middle and high via the ambiguous-LEVEL path — no real in-person bell-to-bell day to sample. Excluded via a new `_virtual_ids()` check (`VIRTUAL_TEXT` in `{"Exclusively virtual", "Primarily virtual"}`; `"Supplemental Virtual"` schools are NOT excluded). Separately, West Bonner County ID's IDAHO HILL and PRIEST RIVER ELEMENTARY (both `PK-06`) were wrongly rescued into middle by the **any-overlap fallback** (for districts whose spans don't form a clean partition) — that fallback still used plain `bands_for()`, which treats grade 6 as inherently part of middle's nominal 6-8 range, the exact dilution shape already fixed for the clean-partition path but never carried over here. Fixed with `bands_for_rescue()` (a school must reach grade 7 to count as touching middle); re-running it surfaced the identical latent bug in an existing test fixture (Breathitt County KY).
- **Cross-band overlap minimization:** a school spanning multiple bands (a genuine K-8, K-12, or "Middle/High"-combined school — what's left *after* LEVEL-primary classification above removes the false dilution cases) can satisfy more than one band's `bands_for()` classification, so naive independent-per-band sampling can draw the *same* school into multiple bands' selections even when enough distinct single-band schools exist. Fixed by processing a district's bands **most-constrained-first** (ascending by candidate-pool size): each band samples from schools not yet claimed by an earlier-processed band first, only falling back to reuse an already-claimed school if the unclaimed pool can't fill the cap. Validated on real data (Fairbanks AK, 0200600): independent sampling produced 3 overlapping schools between elementary and middle; most-constrained-first reduced this to 1 unavoidable overlap (forced because the high band's full 9-school census left middle only 11 unclaimed against its cap of 12).
- When a band's candidate pool exceeds the cap, the 12 (or fewer, after exclusion) are a **seeded random sample** — `seed = f"{batch_id}:{district_id}:{band}"`. No signal exists yet at queue-time for which schools are "better" to sample, so an unbiased subsample is the most defensible default (same logic as the corpus-wide 95/5 sampling theory below).
- **NCES school-count denominator, captured by raw `LEVEL` (added 2026-06-25):** the batch records, per district, `nces_school_counts: {total, by_level}` — the count of schools meeting our eligibility (the same open · regular · non-virtual · non-preschool filter the selection uses, now factored into a shared `school_sampling._eligible()` predicate so the denominator and the band index can never disagree — `total` equals `school_index()`'s distinct-school count), grouped by the **raw `ccd_sch` `LEVEL`** field (Elementary/Middle/High/Secondary/Other/Not reported/…). This is a *denominator/provenance* field, not a selection input: it is **our-criteria count, deliberately NOT `ccd_lea`'s self-reported figure** and NOT the derived 3-band assignment — the cap of 12/band means "we have data for k schools" must never be read as "the district *has* k schools," so the true count travels with the batch. Consumed downstream by Stage 5's `labeled_topology` (e.g. `single_school` = NCES total 1; `incomplete_coverage` = one school-bell target while NCES total > 1) and the eventual targeted-vs-got funnel. Stamped with `nces_year` at the batch level for provenance — a re-ingest after a new NCES release won't silently shift a historical determination.

**Output** — `data/acquisition/queue/batch_NNNNN.json` (5-digit, zero-padded — e.g. `batch_00001.json` — covers the unlikely case of needing one batch per individual US school district), one file per batch. Structured targeting parameters only (district identity, enrollment, claimed bands, the `nces_school_counts` denominator above, and per-band school lists with `n_candidates`/`n_selected` for reviewer transparency; `nces_year` at the batch level) — **no prompt text**. Prompt construction belongs to Stage 2, since Python can't spawn the Haiku WebSearch subagent that Wave 1 discovery needs anyway (agent-in-the-loop, see Stage 2 below) — baking prompts into Stage 1's output would couple queue review to discovery-prompt wording, a different kind of review than "are these the right districts/schools." Schema reference: `data/acquisition/queue/batch.example.json`.

**Checkpoint A (human review)** happens here, **out-of-band** — no `approved`/`reviewed_by` fields in the batch JSON for now (matches the project's current high-touch ramp-up posture; revisit if/when a machine-checkable gate is wanted). The reviewer reads the batch file, confirms the districts/bands/schools are sensible, and gives a verbal/chat go-ahead before Stage 2 runs.

**Status registry** (`data/acquisition/status/district_status.json`, module `infrastructure/acquisition/common/district_status.py`) — a single JSON dict keyed by `district_id` (structure prioritized over scan-ability; this is process input read by every future batch-build to exclude already-attempted districts, not primarily a human-read record). Updated at **every** stage 1-9 as a district progresses, with a per-district `history[]` trail so a human can see why a district landed where it did without cross-referencing batch files. Lives under `data/acquisition/status/`, not nested under `queue/`, since "acquisition" is the umbrella term for the whole 9-stage process and every stage writes to it. **Pre-queue exclusions are deliberately not recorded here** — see filter list above. Schema reference: `data/acquisition/status/district_status.example.json`. Replaces the directory-presence heuristic previously used in `training_batch.py` (archived, see *Key files*).

### 2 · Discovery — designed 2026-06-23 (full session, not yet built or run)

Discovery is a **recall** problem (find *a* schedule page; capture verifies it), so we run tools in **cost-ascending waves** and stop once a district is satisfied — we do **not** run all tools on every district. This design supersedes both `discover.py` (district-level, Perplexity+OpenRouter, reads the old `ground_truth_manifest.json`) and `per_school_run.py` (per-school, but *also* reads the old manifest and rebuilds its own roster from raw NCES CSV via plain `bands_for()`) — **neither consumes Stage 1's `batch_NNNNN.json`**, so neither can be run as-is without silently discarding every CP-A fix from Stage 1 (virtual-school exclusion, CTC exclusion, `recursive_band_groups()`, the 12-per-band cap, cross-band dedup). The `.claude/skills/per-school-acquire*` skills built on top of them are likewise obsolete. Reusable utility pieces from `discover.py` (`host_of()`, `gate()`, `openrouter_search()`, `rank_key()`) carry forward; the manifest-reading and roster-rebuilding do not.

**Input — read, never recompute.** `data/acquisition/queue/batch_NNNNN.json`, the CP-A-approved batch. Per district: `domain` and the full `schools_by_band` (school_id/name/level/gslo/gshi for every targeted school) come straight from the batch file. Stage 2 must never re-derive band membership from raw NCES CSVs — that would re-introduce every dilution/CTC/virtual/grade-13 bug Stage 1 just fixed.

**Orchestration model.** A script reads the batch JSON and drives the whole stage:
1. **Reconciliation pass (before any searching starts)** — for every district in the batch, check whether `data/raw/lea-website-captures/<district_id>_<slug>/discovery.json` already exists.
   - Exists, registry doesn't reflect `furthest_stage >= 2` → **reconcile the registry up** to match disk; skip dispatching a subagent for this district (already done, don't redo it — same "don't silently retry" principle as Stage 1's `already_attempted()`).
   - Doesn't exist → proceed with discovery for this district.
   - **Registry claims `furthest_stage >= 2` but the file doesn't exist** → **stop the entire run and fail loudly.** This is a control failure (lost data, wrong path, bad migration), not routine drift — continuing to process other districts under a possibly-compromised assumption risks propagating whatever caused it. Requires human investigation before any further processing.
2. **Wave 1 — Claude WebSearch.** One Haiku subagent **per district** (never multiple districts in one subagent — avoids context collision), looping internally over that district's schools. Domain-scoped (`allowed_domains` from the batch's `domain` field; unscoped if blank). Query per school: `"{school} {state} bell schedule start and end times"` — no explicit school-year reference; rely on WebSearch's own recency plus domain scoping. **The subagent returns a single, strictly-formatted, machine-parseable JSON result** (not prose for the orchestrator to read and transcribe) — the same "don't nest one probabilistic process's output inside another's interpretation" principle that already governs Wave 2 below, applied to Wave 1's own handoff.
3. **Deterministic script, not a subagent, for everything else:** gate Wave 1's raw URLs (reject news/aggregator domains, off-domain misses — `gate()`); check for a **residual** — any school left with zero kept candidates after gating. **If the residual is empty (Wave 1 + gating satisfied every school in the district), skip Wave 2 entirely — no OpenRouter call is made.** Only when a residual exists does Wave 2 (OpenRouter `gpt-4o-mini-search`) run, scoped strictly to those unsatisfied schools, never the whole roster. Either way: flatten + dedup candidates across schools by normalized URL (this collapses a hub page into one capture target shared across schools automatically — see Topology below); write the district's output (next); update the registry.
4. **Manual flag** for any school still unsatisfied after both waves — assume Claude WebSearch and OpenRouter both genuinely found nothing. Recorded as a registry outcome only (one file to scan for fallout across every stage, not a separate flagged-list file) — but `discovery.json` (below) keeps the full per-school detail, since a school we attempted to discover should always have a recorded outcome.

**Concurrency.** Start at **2 concurrent subagents** for the initial shakeout (small failure surface while real-world bugs surface); ramp to **3–4** once stable. No documented hard ceiling found — a third-party blog's claimed Claude Code concurrency cap could not be verified against official docs and should be treated with skepticism. The real constraints are account-wide API rate limits (RPM/ITPM/OTPM, tier-dependent) and the WebSearch tool's own `too_many_requests` under sustained heavy load — watch for those signals and back off rather than targeting a fixed steady-state number.

**Output location — `data/raw/lea-website-captures/<district_id>_<slug>/`, not `data/acquisition/`.** This is deliberate: `data/acquisition/` holds our own pipeline process state (queue, status registry); `data/raw/` holds evidence pulled from the outside world (NCES downloads today, now also search results and captured pages) — and CLAUDE.md's existing **"never modify files in `data/raw/`"** rule applies here with the same force discovery output is write-once. Slug = lowercase, non-alphanumeric→underscore, collapsed, truncated to ~40 chars (the district_id prefix is the real disambiguator; the slug is for human readability). Capture's own per-district artifacts (screenshots, PDFs, OCR text) will land in the same directory once Stage 3 exists, rather than introducing a second per-district location.

**Files, written exactly once per district, atomically, only after the full chain (Wave 1 + Wave 2 + gating + flatten) completes — never partially, and never by a subagent directly:**
- `discovery.json` — the full audit trail: every school in the targeted roster (found *or* flagged — if it went into discovery, we should know what happened to it), its query, raw Wave-1 URLs, raw Wave-2 URLs (where invoked), and the gate decision (kept/rejected + reason) for each.
- `candidates.json` — the flattened, deduped, capture-ready URL list (naturally omits schools with nothing found).

**Redo policy — versioned, never overwritten, and always manual.** A redo never overwrites `discovery.json` in place; it writes a new timestamped/versioned file, preserving the full attempt history (the project's transparency/audit principle). Stage 2 never automatically re-attempts a district whose directory already has a `discovery.json` — the existence-check above is what normally prevents this; redoing one is a deliberate, explicitly-invoked override only, same philosophy as Stage 1's "don't silently retry failures."

**Registry semantics — the filesystem is authoritative; the registry is a cache of it, reconciled from it, never the reverse.** Only the single-threaded orchestrating script ever reads or writes `district_status.json` — never a subagent directly — to avoid race conditions on the shared JSON file under concurrent dispatch. One registry write per district, at actual completion (not an interim "started" marker — there's nothing meaningful to reconcile against a half-finished state, since the file write itself only happens once everything is assembled). Outcome vocabulary (starting point, expected to evolve): `found_all` / `found_partial` / `manual_flag_all` — full per-school detail lives in `discovery.json`, not the registry.

**Topology classification — dropped, deliberately, for now.** There isn't enough signal to classify a district as `hub` / `per_school` / `none` reliably from search results alone, before any page content has been read. The practical cost is small: deduping candidate URLs by normalized URL already collapses a hub page into one shared capture target regardless of any topology label, so the "capture the hub once" efficiency isn't lost — only the *future* possibility of using known topology to refine Stage 2's own queries (once CP-B capture results are in hand) is deferred, not lost, since the raw per-school URL audit trail in `discovery.json` survives for that later analysis.

**Stage 2 → Stage 3 boundary.** Stage 2 ends at producing its own finalized output (registry reconciled/updated, `discovery.json`/`candidates.json` written). Stage 3 is now fully designed (see below) but not yet built; once it is, the rest of the chain (gate → Wave 2 → flatten → registry write → invoke `capture_discovery.mjs`) is pure deterministic script with no further agent involvement, so it can run as one continuous chain immediately after Wave 1's subagents return. No separate human checkpoint sits between Discovery and Capture in the current 3-checkpoint model. **CP-B's placement, settled 2026-06-23: after Stage 5 (Local filtering), before Stage 6 (Hand to OpenRouter)** — the human reviews what's actually about to be sent to the paid extraction council, not raw discovered URLs or raw Stage 3 captures. (Earlier framing in this doc placed CP-B right after Capture; that's superseded by this placement.)

### 3 · Capture — *tiered* (local Playwright) — designed 2026-06-23, not yet built

**Input.** Reads each district's `candidates.json` (Stage 2's deduped, capture-ready URL list) from `data/raw/lea-website-captures/<district_id>_<slug>/`. `candidates.json` itself is **never modified** by Capture — write-once, same as Stage 2.

**Reconciliation, before any fetching starts — the same filesystem-is-truth principle as Stage 2, applied to a new stage.** A district's `captures.json` existing in its directory IS "Stage 3 done" for that district; the registry is a cache of that fact, reconciled from disk, never the reverse. Registry-ahead-of-disk (registry claims `furthest_stage>=3` but `captures.json` doesn't exist) is a control failure — stop the entire run, same as Stage 2's reconcile().

**Per-candidate branch logic** (applies to every URL in `candidates.json`, and to any *emergent* candidate found during HTML rendering — see below):

1. **Google Drive / Docs / Sheets / Slides** (`drive.google.com` / `docs.google.com` host) — checked *first*, before the generic branches below, since these need their own handling rather than being captured as generic HTML or generic binary downloads:
   - **Tier 1 — unauthenticated export URL**, for a recognized single-file/doc/sheet/slide link pattern (`file/d/…`, `document/d/…`, `spreadsheets/d/…`, `presentation/d/…`, `open?id=…`). Confirmed via research (Gemini MCP + a citation-backed Perplexity cross-check) this still works reliably today for "anyone with the link" content, no authentication needed:
     - **Docs → export as PDF *and* Markdown.** Markdown is the cheap win: a born-digital Doc's Markdown export is already clean plain text, skipping `pdftotext` entirely — a candidate for the council's cheapest reading tier.
     - **Slides → export as PDF.**
     - **Sheets → export as CSV *and* PDF.** PDF is the safety net for the edge case where someone has pasted an image into a sheet — CSV alone would silently lose it, same failure shape as an image-only Doc.
     - **Generic Drive-hosted file (not a native Doc/Sheet/Slide) → direct download** via the `uc?export=download&id=` transform.
     - All formats applicable to a given link type are captured unconditionally — the same "always capture everything available, let later stages decide" principle already applied to `page.pdf()` below, not a conditional trigger.
   - **Tier 2 — OAuth-authenticated Google Drive API**, used when (a) Tier 1 fails for an individual file (e.g., an interstitial instead of the expected content-type), or (b) the link is a **folder**, which Tier 1 can never handle — confirmed via research that `files.list` always requires OAuth or a service account, even for fully public folders; there is no API-key-only path. `files.list` enumerates the folder's contents (recursing into nested folders, bounded — exact depth/count cap to be set when we actually hit a real nested-folder case, not designed in the abstract); `files.get`/`files.export` retrieves each child's content, routed back through this same per-type export logic (PDF+Markdown for a Doc child, etc.).
   - **No Playwright-preview tier, no Gemini-API tier.** Both dropped from `google_drive_handler.py`'s original 3-tier design: Playwright-rendering Drive's web UI depended on the now-dead Express microservice and fights a UI Google didn't design for this; Gemini would only end up calling the same Drive API that already failed, buying nothing. If OAuth+Drive API can't retrieve something, that's a capture failure, not an interpretation problem.
   - **If the OAuth/Drive-API path itself fails** (invalid/revoked/expired token, genuinely inaccessible content, quota) for a specific candidate: record it (`err: "needs_oauth_reauth"` in that candidate's `captures.json` record) and **move on to the next candidate — this does NOT halt the run.** Unlike the Stage 2 billing CONTROL FAILURE (where one failure means every subsequent call fails identically), one stuck Drive item says nothing about the rest of the batch. The district's registry outcome rolls this up (e.g. `captured_partial`, `notes: "1 candidate needs_oauth_reauth"`) — **the registry holds a status, never a live array of open issues** (a recipe for sync bugs); a human generates the actual triage list on demand by scanning `captures.json` files for that error string when ready to act on it, not by reading an accumulating structure inside the registry itself.
   - **One-time human setup, not runtime behavior:** a GCP project with an OAuth "installed app" client, with the **consent screen explicitly moved out of "Testing" publishing status** — confirmed via research this is the actual trigger for forced-short (7-day) refresh tokens, not "personal/unverified app" status as such (a claim Gemini made that a citation-backed Perplexity follow-up could not confirm in Google's actual OAuth docs — flagging the discrepancy rather than picking whichever answer was convenient). Out of Testing mode, a one-time interactive authorization by the operator produces a refresh token intended to remain valid indefinitely (until revoked), enabling fully unattended calls afterward — this should make the "human must be present" case rare, not a routine per-run dependency. Token stored locally, gitignored, same handling as `config/secrets.local.json`.
2. **Direct PDF/image fetch** (non-Google URLs only) — plain HTTP GET (`fetch()` → `writeFileSync`), an unmodified byte-for-byte copy. Preserves the original file's structure and text layer exactly because nothing is transformed. **Never** `page.pdf()` on a URL that's already a PDF — Chromium's PDF viewer rendering a re-rendered PDF of itself is a lossy, pointless round-trip.
3. **Generic HTML render** (everything else):
   - `page.goto(url, { waitUntil: 'networkidle' })`, then the existing `page.waitForTimeout(2500)`.
   - **Modal dismissal**, sequenced *after* that 2.5s wait (giving slow, deliberately-delayed cookie/consent banners a window to actually render before dismissal is attempted) — ported from `capturer.ts`'s `dismissModals()`: CSS-injection to hide overlays globally → click known dismiss-button selectors → DOM removal as last resort. Verified `capturer.ts` is pure Playwright with zero coupling to the dead Crawlee/Express-server architecture, so this is a near-verbatim port, not a rewrite.
   - **innerText across all frames → `.txt`** (existing).
   - **Full-page screenshot → `.png`** (existing).
   - **`page.pdf()` → `.pdf`, unconditionally, for every HTML page captured** — no "is this multi-column" detection trigger. This deliberately resolves the Reader-Routing spec's previously-unsolved "Tier 1→2 escalation trigger" (detecting "text present but structure lost" was left open as genuinely hard) by removing the need for a trigger at all: capture both representations every time: local compute is free, and the decision of which one to actually use moves downstream to Stage 4/7, which have more signal to make it well.
   - **Emergent candidates**: while the page is rendered, scan the DOM for anchor tags whose link text or `href` matches the existing `SCHED_KW` keyword list (`bell`, `schedule`, `hours`, `start-time`, `start_time`, `daily-schedule`, `times`, `school-day`, `schoolday` — the same list `discover.py` already uses, not a new one). Any match becomes a new candidate task, **exactly one hop, never recursively chained** (this is not a revival of the abandoned blind-crawl design — it's gated on the same keyword signal already proven useful elsewhere, triggered only from a page Capture is already rendering). Recorded in `captures.json` with `source: "emergent"` and `found_on: <the page's URL>` for traceability, then processed through this same branch logic like any other candidate. **This path is explicitly intended to catch CDN-hosted materials, not just Drive/Docs links** — an on-domain page Discovery *did* find can easily link to a bell-schedule PDF hosted off-domain on a CMS/CDN host (Finalsite, BoardDocs, SchoolWires/Blackboard, an S3 bucket, etc. — the same `CMS_HOSTS` set `discover.py`'s `gate()` already special-cases) that Discovery's domain-scoped search would never have surfaced directly. The branch logic already routes such a link correctly once found (it lands on the direct PDF/image fetch branch like any other binary target) — the requirement here is that the emergent-candidate scan and its test coverage must not be written narrowly around the Drive/Docs case alone; a CDN-hosted-PDF emergent candidate is a first-class case to test, not an incidental side effect.

**Output, written once per district (matching Stage 2's write pattern):**
- `captures/` subdirectory, with **one further subdirectory per captured URL, named by `md5(url).slice(0,10)`** — not flat hash-prefixed files sharing one folder. The point of hashing the URL was always to let a human open one folder and see everything for that one page (`page.txt`/`page.png`/`page.pdf`, or a Drive export's `pdf.pdf`/`md.md`/etc.) without mentally regrouping files by matching prefixes — this was misdocumented and mis-implemented as flat hash-prefixed files on the first pass (caught by the user reviewing real output, fixed before the real batch ran for real). Stage 4 reuses this same per-URL directory to write its own output alongside Stage 3's files (see below).
- `captures.json` — per-candidate record (url, hash, tools/source/found_on, ok, kind, final_url, files, err, **fingerprint**) — a **separate file from `candidates.json`**, not a mutation of it, so Discovery's output stays untouched.

**Hosting/CMS fingerprint (per record, added 2026-06-24).** Every record carries a `fingerprint` block of **raw signals only** about how/where the page is hosted — recorded at the **URL level** (school subdomains routinely run a different platform than the district root), gathered for free from data the capture was already holding and discarding (the `goto`/`fetch` Response headers, plus one extra DOM `evaluate` riding alongside the existing emergent-anchor scan). Fields: `final_host`, `server`, `powered_by`, `cdn_hints[]` (presence-detected: cloudflare/aws/vercel/github-pages/netlify/fastly/azure/google), `meta_generator`, `resource_hosts[]` (distinct off-domain script/link/img hostnames — the vendor-CDN signal, own-host dropped, capped at 20), `js_dependent` (a coarse proxy: served-HTML text near-empty but rendered text substantial → content came from JS; `null` for non-HTML records), and `cms_hint` (the **one** cheap inline classification — a host-suffix match against the same `CMS_HOSTS` set `discover.py` uses, `null` on no match). **Deliberately facts, not classification:** real CMS identification is a later pure function over these signals, refinable retroactively without re-capture — the same "capture facts, decide downstream" invariant as the rest of the pipeline. Direct PDF/image/Drive records get a reduced fingerprint (host + headers + cdn_hints only; no DOM fields).
- **Backfill for parity:** `capture_discovery.mjs backfill-fingerprints <root> [conc]` re-visits each existing `ok` record, computes **only** the fingerprint, and patches it into `captures.json` (versioned-redo write) — does **not** re-render/re-save `page.*` or touch any Stage 4 output, so `processed.json` stays valid (Stage 4 never reads fingerprints). Run live 2026-06-24 against all 12 `batch_00001` districts: 150/150 records fingerprinted, 0 errors.
- **First-run finding (the feature earning its keep):** `cms_hint` was `null` on all 150 — *correctly*, because the real platforms in this sample weren't in the discovery-era `CMS_HOSTS` list. The raw `resource_hosts` revealed the actual landscape: **SharpSchool** (`*.sharpschool.com`, 51 records — the plurality), **Apptegy/Thrillshare** (`*.apptegy.net` + `core-docs.s3.amazonaws.com`, ~24), and **Educational Networks** (`*.educationalnetworks.net`, 25). **Resolved 2026-06-24 (Open decision #8):** the three vendors were human-approved and added to `CMS_HOSTS` (also closing a possible Stage 2 discovery-recall gap), under a standing governance rule that every `CMS_HOSTS` addition is human-in-the-loop and school-district-vendors-only (`amazonaws.com` deliberately *not* added despite `core-docs.s3.amazonaws.com` appearing — general hosting, pollution risk). See Open decision #8.

**DOM segmentation — header/footer/nav vs. main content (PLANNED 2026-06-25, not yet built).** The single highest-leverage Stage 5 signal fix is **de-chroming**: CMS chrome (a global footer's "Building Hours 7:15–3:15", a school-switcher `nav` listing every school, a footer carrying board/athletics/calendar links on *every* page) injects false signal — fake start/end proximity-pairs (→ false-positive tiers), inflated `roster_school_names_hit` (→ false `hub` topology, e.g. Marion), and negative keywords that drag real schedule pages down. **The reliable fix must happen at Stage 3, at render time, because that is the only place the DOM/CSS exists** — we deliberately persist `innerText` only (never raw HTML, see the fingerprint constraint above), so by Stage 4 the structure that identifies "this is a footer" is already gone. Design, mirroring the fingerprint pattern exactly:
  - **Segment, don't strip.** During the same render, capture `innerText` of the structural landmarks (`<header>`/`<footer>`/`<nav>` + ARIA `[role="banner"]`/`[role="contentinfo"]`/`[role="navigation"]`) as **separate representations** alongside the untouched full `page.txt`: `page.main.txt` (body minus chrome — the clean signal), `page.header.txt`, `page.footer.txt`, `page.nav.txt`. The full `page.txt` is **always kept** — segmentation is additive and best-effort, never lossy.
  - **Raw segments, not classification** — same invariant as fingerprinting. Stage 5 computes signals **per representation** and tiers on `main` only, so chrome can no longer contaminate the verdict; the chrome representations stay fully inspectable and are **screened separately** (a footer start/end pair + `office hours`/`building hours` ⇒ the `building_hours_visible` flag; + `school hours`/`dismissal` and not in a board/sports nav ⇒ a candidate school-hours signal). Header/footer are kept precisely because the real **school hours** sometimes live there, not only the confounding building hours.
  - **Graceful degradation + backfill.** When no landmarks are found (a CMS using `<div class="footer">` rather than semantic/ARIA markup), `main` = full page and the chrome reps are empty — never worse than today. For `batch_00001` (already captured), a **`backfill-segments` mode** mirrors `backfill-fingerprints`: re-visit each `ok` HTML record, run only the segmentation `evaluate`, write the segment files (versioned-redo), touch nothing Stage 4 produced. For future batches it rides the capture for free. (A complementary, no-re-capture corroborator — cross-page boilerplate detection, text identical across many of a district's pages ≈ chrome — is a Stage-5 signal, weaker, not the primary mechanism.) **Open:** the exact landmark-selector list and the promote-vs-suppress screening rule are to be pinned before building.

**Registry outcome (Stage 3, same `record_stage()` API as Stages 1-2, no schema changes needed):** `captured_all` / `captured_partial` / `capture_failed_all`, with a short `notes` summary for anything flagged (e.g. `needs_oauth_reauth`) — full per-candidate detail lives only in `captures.json`.

- Code: `infrastructure/scraper/capture_discovery.mjs` (active, to be extended); `capturer.ts` (modal dismissal + `page.pdf()` options, to be ported in, not run as-is — confirmed it has zero dependency on the dead Crawlee/Express/Ollama design despite its file header's stale comment); `infrastructure/scripts/enrich/google_drive_handler.py` (Tier 1 export-URL logic salvageable; its Playwright-preview and Gemini tiers dropped, see above).
- **Superseded:** `mapper.ts`'s `PlaywrightCrawler` usage — confirmed to be the literal abandoned Jan-2026 blind-site-mapping design, not imported by anything in the active pipeline. Do not revive.

### 4 · Local processing — designed, built, and run live 2026-06-23 (150/150 records processed across all 12 `batch_00001` districts)

**Purpose.** Reduce what Stage 7 (the paid extraction council) has to read, and what it costs — Stage 4 spends free local compute so OpenRouter never pays to discover that a captured page has no usable text. Its only question per captured URL is *is there any machine-readable text here at all* — not whether that text is actually about a bell schedule (Stage 5's job) and not which reader produces the most perfectly-structured table (Stage 7's council, a language model, is better positioned to disentangle merged/spanning cells than deterministic code is — Stage 4 doesn't need to solve table reconstruction, just surface enough signal).

**Input.** Walks each district's directory under `data/raw/lea-website-captures/<district_id>_<slug>/`, reading `captures.json` (Stage 3's output). Never modified — same write-once principle as every prior stage's relationship to the stage before it.

**Reconciliation, before any per-district processing starts — two checks, both fail loud at the same severity:**
1. **Registry consistency**, same shape as Stage 2/3: a district's `processed.json` existing on disk IS "Stage 4 done" for that district; the registry is a cache, reconciled from disk, never the reverse. Registry-ahead-of-disk (claims `furthest_stage>=4`, `processed.json` doesn't exist) halts the entire run — a control failure. Disk-ahead-of-registry (file exists, registry hasn't caught up) reconciles up silently; the fix for a behind registry is just to update it, never to halt or re-process.
2. **File-existence consistency** — new, finer-grained than any prior stage's check: for every `ok: true` record in `captures.json`, every filename listed in its `files` map must actually exist in that record's `captures/<hash>/` directory. A mismatch halts the entire run, same severity as registry-ahead-of-disk — `captures.json` claiming a file exists when it doesn't means something is structurally broken (a partial write, a manual deletion, a Stage 3 bug), not a normal content failure, so it isn't worked around or skipped. `ok: false` records (`capture_failed`, `needs_oauth_reauth`) have `files: {}` by design — not an inconsistency, simply skipped by this check.

**Per-URL-directory processing — run every kept tool against every applicable input, always. No waterfall, no gating on prior success.** This superseded an earlier "stop at first usable representation" design (2026-06-23 morning) after a real test on Longfellow Elementary's `page.pdf` showed the PDF branch surfacing a cleaner `8:20-School Begins`/`3:20-School Ends` pair via `pdftotext` *even though* the existing `.txt` already looked usable — proving the short-circuit was throwing away real signal, not just doing redundant work. A follow-up empirical spike (below) confirmed this generalizes, not just an isolated case.

1. **Every PDF** (`page.pdf`, `original.pdf`, or a Drive export's `pdf.pdf`) gets all four kept extraction methods unconditionally: `pdftotext -layout`, `pdfplumber.extract_tables()` (`lines` strategy), `camelot.read_pdf(flavor="stream")`, `camelot.read_pdf(flavor="hybrid")`. Each is evaluated independently against the usable-text bar (below) and kept regardless of whether another method on the same PDF already cleared it. `pdfplumber`/`camelot` output is rendered as real **Markdown-table syntax** (header row + `---` separator + data rows), not the ad hoc pipe-join the exploratory spike used — preserves row/column structure unambiguously. Kept as plain `.txt` (not `.md`) — the file extension is invisible to the model either way, since the content gets pasted into an OpenRouter chat-completion prompt string, never uploaded as a file; `.txt` avoids mixed-extension clutter in `captures/<hash>/` for no functional gain.
2. **Every image** gets Tesseract OCR unconditionally, kept as up to **three separate, distinctly-named representations** since they're genuinely different inputs that can disagree: `tesseract_screenshot` (the existing `page.png`, html-kind browser screenshot), `tesseract_image` (a direct image download, `kind: "image"`), and `tesseract_raster` (a fresh rasterization of any PDF present, via `pdftoppm -r 200` — porting the working `_ocr_pdf` pattern from `reading.py`/`gt_propose.py`, since tesseract cannot read a PDF directly). Rasterized pages are **persisted**, not ephemeral (a deliberate departure from `reading.py`'s `tempfile.TemporaryDirectory()` precedent) — named `raster_p-<N>.png` (pdftoppm's own numbering convention) so they never collide with Stage 3's `page.png`, a genuinely different rendering path kept as a separate OCR input, not a substitute.
3. **`.txt`/`.md`/`.csv`** (already written by Stage 3 or a Drive export) is evaluated against the same usable-text bar as everything else, with no special-cased priority — **referenced, never rewritten**.
4. **Every attempted representation gets an entry** in the output — success, below-bar, *or* errored (e.g. a malformed PDF camelot can't parse) — never silently omitted. Completeness, not just success, is the audit bar, same principle as `discovery.json`. Each entry carries its own `usable` boolean, not just a record-level rollup.
5. **The usable-text bar** (recognizable text, not garbled binary — non-trivial length plus a printable-character/encoding-sanity check, starting threshold 120 characters per `reading.py`'s existing `PDF_MIN_TEXT_CHARS` precedent) is evaluated **per representation**, independently — it decides whether that one representation counts as usable, not whether to attempt the next tool. Deliberately **not** the time-count/keyword check Stage 5 uses for relevance (see the Marion ISD example below).
6. **If literally nothing clears the bar** across every representation: the record is `usable: false`. **Stage 4 does not decide whether to escalate to vision** — that belongs to Stage 6/7's handoff.

**Tool roster, resolved 2026-06-23 by an empirical spike against all 150 real captured PDFs across `batch_00001`** — not decided from research alone (`data/benchmark_results/stage4_pdf_tool_spike/run_spike.py`, deps in `requirements.txt`):

| Kept | Found ≥1 plausible time | Avg times found | Beat the existing `.txt` baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tesseract` | 60.0% (highest) | 10.5 | 15/150 |
| `pdftotext -layout` | 58.7% | 10.5 | 8/150 |
| `camelot` `stream` | 51.3% | 7.5 | 8/150 |
| `pdfplumber` `lines` | 42.7% | 7.2 | **30/150 (highest)** |
| `camelot` `hybrid` | 42.0% | **11.4 (highest)** | 16/150 |

**Dropped, with evidence, not just superseded research claims:** PyMuPDF/`fitz` (14.7% hit rate — confirmed low marginal value over pdfplumber, consistent with a PyMuPDF maintainer's own confirmation the two tools "have the same root"); `pdfplumber` `text` strategy (33.3% hit rate despite extracting *more* raw characters than `lines` — confirmed garbling: on Longfellow's `page.pdf` it produced word-chopped fragments with Private-Use-Area icon-webfont glyphs bleeding into the output); Camelot `network`/`lattice` (24.0%/20.7% — `stream`/`hybrid` cover the same ground better); `img2table` bordered and borderless (2.0%/23.3% — bordered mode found a time in **zero** of the 140 `page.pdf` captures, exactly confirming the predicted weakness that CSS border strokes don't survive OpenCV rasterization the way Word/Excel's vector lines do; also the most complex to wire, needing an OCR backend internally for no payoff).

**Heavy ML-model document tools (Docling, EasyOCR, PaddleOCR) were installed, timed, then deliberately rejected and uninstalled — not a "maybe later," a closed decision.** Each costs 13-56 seconds *per page* (measured) for model inference — at full corpus scale this is hours-to-days of compute for a stage whose whole purpose is to avoid spending money on Stage 7, not to spend large amounts of local wall-clock instead. The user's framing: any input that genuinely needs heavy-model-level understanding should go to the **paid vision council (Stage 7)**, not a slow local model standing in for it — the same tradeoff that already retired local Ollama for this project (see CLAUDE.md). Tesseract remains the only OCR engine.

**Spot-checking the metric, not just trusting it — two real examples from the spike, both worth keeping in mind:**
- **Real win:** Stroudsburg's `page.pdf` — `camelot_hybrid` recovered a clean `Period | Start Time | End Time | Length` table (`Period 1 | 7:30 AM | 8:12 AM | 42 min`...) that beat the existing `.txt` (88 → 206 times found), because the page has multiple schedule-variant tables the flattened innerText had muddled together.
- **Real caution:** Pittsylvania's single biggest "win" by raw time-count turned out to be a **Student Handbook PDF**, not a schedule page — `camelot_hybrid` was mis-firing table-detection on welcome-letter prose, and the time-count regex was catching stray digit patterns. This is the same "schedule buried in a handbook" failure mode already named in Deferred hard input cases, below — confirms a raw time-count is supporting evidence, not proof of quality, and Stage 5's relevance/keyword check still matters even with richer per-tool input.

**Known, deliberately deferred: byte-identical PDFs captured under different URLs are not deduped.** Confirmed real on Marion ISD (`1918690_marion_independent_school_district`): an emergent candidate found via a `5il.co` shortlink and its resolved CDN URL captured the exact same PDF bytes (verified by hash) into two separate `captures/<hash>/` directories. Deduping would require a cross-directory content-hash pass over the whole district before processing any single directory — real added complexity for a benefit that's currently negligible (local pdftotext/pdfplumber/tesseract runs are cheap; this is one occasional redundant run, not a scaling problem). Watch-item, same posture as Drive Tier 2 — revisit only if duplicate-emergent-capture volume becomes a real cost at scale.

**Worked example — Marion ISD, illustrating the Stage 4 / Stage 5 boundary.** Two of its captured pages (`aa1d5cfb30`, `d9e387ea42` — HTML wrapper pages for the VMS/MHS bell schedule; the actual PDFs live elsewhere as separate CDN-hosted emergent candidates) have ~2,600 bytes of real, readable `page.txt` content but `text_times: 0`. Under Stage 4's bar that's `usable: true` — it's genuine text. Stage 5's relevance filter is what correctly rejects them as not actually being about a schedule. That's the intended division of labor, not a near-miss the design should try to close.

**Output, written once per district (matching Stages 2/3's write pattern):** `processed.json` — never amends `captures.json` (write-once, same as every prior stage's relationship to the stage before it). A **bare array** (matching `captures.json`'s shape, not `discovery.json`'s metadata-wrapper shape), minimal by design — just `url`/`hash` as the join key back to `captures.json`, not denormalized, since duplicating fields risks drift if `captures.json` is ever redone independently. Per record, a **list** of representations (changed from a single winning `source`/`text_file` once "run everything, always" replaced the waterfall), each carrying its own `usable` flag and an `error` field when a tool attempt failed outright:
```json
{ "url": "...", "hash": "...", "usable": true,
  "texts": [
    { "source": "txt", "text_file": "page.txt", "n_chars": 2846, "n_times": 4, "usable": true },
    { "source": "pdftotext", "text_file": "pdftotext.txt", "n_chars": 1041, "n_times": 3, "usable": true },
    { "source": "pdfplumber_lines", "text_file": "pdfplumber_lines.txt", "n_chars": 792, "n_times": 7, "usable": true },
    { "source": "camelot_stream", "text_file": null, "n_chars": 0, "n_times": 0, "usable": false, "error": "ValueError: max() iterable argument is empty" },
    { "source": "camelot_hybrid", "text_file": "camelot_hybrid.txt", "n_chars": 1103, "n_times": 11, "usable": true },
    { "source": "tesseract_screenshot", "text_file": "tesseract_screenshot.txt", "n_chars": 2701, "n_times": 2, "usable": true },
    { "source": "tesseract_raster", "text_file": "tesseract_raster.txt", "n_chars": 2710, "n_times": 4, "usable": true }
  ] }
```
`usable` at the record level is "at least one representation cleared the bar" (an OR across the list, not a single source's verdict). `text_file` is a **reference only** — a filename resolved against that record's own `captures/<hash>/` directory, the same convention `captures.json` already uses for `files`; `null` when the attempt errored, since there's nothing to reference. Each representation's actual text lives in its own file, never embedded inline in `processed.json`. Only representations applicable to that record appear in the list (e.g. no PDF-tool entries for a record with no PDF). The comparison/ranking/synthesis *across* these representations — which one to actually trust, whether they agree — is deliberately not Stage 4's job; see the Stage 4/5 boundary note below.

**Redo is versioned, never an overwrite** — matches `discover_stage2.py`'s `write_discovery()` convention exactly: an existing `processed.json` is renamed aside with a UTC timestamp suffix (`processed.<ts>.json`) before the new one is written. `capture_discovery.mjs`'s `captures.json` write was retrofitted to match this same convention in the same round of work (it previously just overwrote in place — a real, now-fixed gap).

**Registry outcome (Stage 4, same `record_stage()` API, no schema changes):** `processed_all` / `processed_partial` / `no_usable_text_any`, written once per district at the conclusion of that district's processing.

**Stage 4 / Stage 5 boundary, restated after the "always run" pivot: Stage 4 is production, Stage 5 is decisions — but a standardized, always-wanted synthesized file is still Stage 4's job, *if* one turns out to exist.** The user's framing: comparing/ranking/filtering across representations belongs to Stage 5, but if a particular synthesis (e.g. a deterministic merge of agreeing representations) turns out to be something every downstream consumer always wants in the same operationalized shape, producing it is production, not a decision, and belongs in Stage 4. **Not decided now** — there's no evidence yet that such a synthesis exists or is worth building; Stage 4 currently produces the raw list of representations + cheap per-representation attributes (`n_chars`, `n_times`) and nothing more. Revisit once Stage 5 has run against real `processed.json` output and we can see whether a recurring synthesis pattern actually emerges. Stage 5's own output is provisionally named **`filtered.json`** (the user's proposal, accepted) — a bare array, same reasoning as `processed.json` given the volume of material a human reviews at CP-B.

**Built and run live 2026-06-23, real result:** `infrastructure/acquisition/stage4_process/process_stage4.py run --all` against all 12 `batch_00001` districts — **150/150 records processed, zero crashes**, 10 districts `processed_all`, 2 `processed_partial` (Stroudsburg, Pittsylvania). Only 4/150 records (2.7%) have no usable representation at all, and all four are explainable, not bugs: three are Pittsylvania Student/Parent Handbook PDFs that captured as genuinely near-empty content (`n_chars=1`, no errors — the capture itself got little/nothing, consistent with the "schedule buried in a handbook" failure mode named in Deferred hard input cases, below), and one is Stroudsburg's already-flagged low-value `cross.jsp` cross-reference link.

Extraction standardizes on **OpenRouter** (`google/gemini-2.5-flash` etc.) — even though the first Flash pass used the native Google API. Inputs: OCR'd text / clean text / PDFs / screenshots per the tier.

### 7 · Extraction — council, **per-school**
- Extract **each school page separately** (not a concatenated district dump — that's the over-stuffing failure mode), pulling the **first-bell START and last-bell END** time per band. **Target = GROSS daily instructional minutes (end − start), bell-to-bell.** We do **NOT** subtract lunch/passing/recess, and we do **NOT** apply assumed deductions — gross is the honest, simple target (it only needs two numbers nearly every schedule states plainly). Net minutes is a deferred future enhancement; "gross-with-assumptions" is dropped (assumed deductions add fake precision). **Ignore early-release/block days** — standard full day only. (Note: our existing GT was already gross — `instructional_minutes` = end − start — so this removes a spec/GT contradiction rather than lowering the bar.)
- **Council = the 6 non-reasoning candidates** (Update 4 of the benchmark doc): default **Gemini 2.5 Flash**; cross-family partners **DeepSeek V3.2 / Mistral Large 2512**; near-free members **Mistral Small 24B / Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite / Qwen3-235B-2507**. Grok 4.3 and Qwen3.7-Max removed (reasoning-token cost 4–70×; not 100% on difficulty>0.70). **Exact membership + decision rule: open.**
- Accuracy context: top models hit **~95–100% on good inputs (difficulty>0.70)** but ~68% on the full 41 — **input quality is the ceiling, not the model.**

### 8 · Aggregation — modal, then mean
Across the sampled schools in a district, the band value is the **modal** (most common) gross minutes; if the mode is inconclusive/uncertain, fall back to the **arithmetic mean** for that band. **Models extract per-school start/end rows; deterministic code computes the mode** — never ask the model to pick the "typical" schedule (that hides the distribution and offloads statistics to the LLM). (`school_schedules` per-school rows → MODE-aggregate to `bell_schedules`; schema ready, migration 016, REQ-042.)

### 9 · Incorporation — fail loud
Write the district band values to the DB. A district where discovery finds nothing or the council can't agree lands as **`method=statutory_fallback`** — **labeled, never counted as enriched** (Rule #6, REQ-024). Coverage ≠ enrichment.

## Extraction council design (stage 7) — candidate configs, to be selected empirically

Grounded in the council research (`docs/technical-notes/LLM_COUNCIL_RESEARCH_2026-06.md`) and the measured leaderboard/costs (`EXTRACTION_BENCHMARK_FINDINGS.md` Update 3–4). **Principles, fixed:**
- **INVARIANT — extractors read TIMES; deterministic code computes MINUTES and the MODE.** A council model only ever returns per-school `{start_time, end_time, grade_level, school_name}` *facts it read from the artifact*. It never computes instructional minutes, never subtracts anything, and never picks a "typical"/modal schedule. All arithmetic (`gross = end − start`) and all aggregation (the per-band mode over schools) are done in Python (`aggregate.py`). This keeps the distribution visible and auditable, and keeps the LLM doing the one thing it's good at (reading) and code doing the one thing it's reliable at (counting). *(Decided 2026-06-21; a prior design let models return pre-aggregated minutes and a triage prompt even asked the model to pick the typical schedule — both removed.)*
- **Consensus is on the per-school (start_time, end_time) PAIR**, evaluated per school within ±15 min, cross-family — NOT on the computed minutes or the band rollup. Two models "agreeing on 380 minutes" via *different* start/end pairs is a false agreement; agreeing on `08:00–14:20` for *this school* is a real one.
- **Consensus must be cross-family.** Agreement only counts between different model families — same-family agreement (two Gemini, two Mistral) is weak evidence (correlated blind spots). Family buckets: **Google** (Flash, Flash-Lite) · **Mistral** (Small 24B, Large 2512) · **DeepSeek** (V3.2) · **Qwen** (235B-2507).
- **The band value is the deterministic MODE** (exact most-common gross over consensus schools; genuine tie between distinct values → arithmetic mean). The mode is the *exact* most-common value, not a tolerance-cluster mean (a cluster-mean bug once turned {380×26, 390×2, 345×1} into 381 instead of 380).
- **Disagreements go to a JUDGE that re-reads the captured page** + applies a plausibility gate (school day ~240–**510** min — the upper bound raised from 480 because real full days run to ~8.5h, e.g. LA at 500; start ~6:30–9:30), not to more voters — a judge can catch a *shared* trio error that voting cannot. Judge is a distinct family from the voters.
- **~3 diverse voters + 1 judge** is the sweet spot; do not call all 6. **Log which models formed the consensus** for each school (DB change — see below) to audit for monoculture.
- **Run the council per *school*, not per district** (concatenating schools is the over-stuffing failure); cheap path per school, escalate only on disagreement; aggregate across schools in stage 8.

**The two candidate configs (A/B — the decision is the measured escalation rate):**

| | **Path 1 — cheap trio** | **Path 2 — accuracy pair** |
|---|---|---|
| Voters | Mistral Small 24B + Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite + Qwen3-235B-2507 | Mistral Large 2512 + Gemini 2.5 Flash |
| Families in accept-path | 3 | 2 |
| Judge (if needed) | DeepSeek V3.2 (4th family) | **DeepSeek V3.2** (3rd family; preferred over Qwen — higher accuracy 66.2% vs 58.1%) |
| Base cost / school | ~$0.0013 | ~$0.0043 (~3×) |
| Voter accuracy (full-41) | 63.5 / 60.8 / 58.1% | 67.6 / 68.9% |
| Expected escalation | higher (weaker voters disagree more on hard inputs) | lower (best models agree-and-right more often) |
| Judge role | tiebreak on a split (1–1–1 or 2–1 cross-family) | **decider on every disagreement** (a 2-voter pair has no internal majority → judge breaks every 1–1 tie) |

**Accept rule (both paths):** accept when the required cross-family voters agree within ±15 min; otherwise invoke the judge; if the judge's value fails the plausibility gate or no defensible value emerges → **statutory fallback** (small/single-school districts where no alternate school exists get flagged for human review instead).

**Why not decided yet:** the winner hinges on the **escalation rate** on *real captured inputs*, which can't be reasoned out — it depends on how often the cheap trio actually converges. That measurement is **blocked on building per-school extract→aggregate** (Open decision #2). Until then, both configs stand as candidates.

## Sampling policy (stage 1/7) — two separate decisions, queue-time settled, extraction-time still open

Computed per-district per-band school counts and the textbook **95% / ±5% finite-population sample size** from NCES `ccd_sch_029_2425` (classifier `infrastructure/acquisition/stage1_queue/school_sampling.py`; bands by grade span `GSLO`-`GSHI`, so a K-8 counts for *both* elementary and middle; open schools only). **Result kills the survey-formula approach:**

- Across **18,158 districts**, 95/±5 sampling = **127,513 band-extractions = 96% of a full census (132,803)** — the finite-population correction saves only ~4%.
- Reason: the corpus is mostly small districts (**median 4 calls / district across 3 bands; p95 = 22**), which get censused regardless. The formula only inflates a few mega-districts (**LA Unified n=496, Broward 286, Orange 254**) — i.e., maximum effort exactly where the marginal school adds least.
- The formula is statistically correct for the *wrong question*: it estimates a worst-case *proportion* (p=0.5), but we want the **modal band-minutes**, and bell times **cluster by district policy** → the mode stabilizes far below the proportion-formula n.

**Decision 1 — queue-time school cap (decided 2026-06-22, see Stage 1 above):** small districts (≤12 schools/band) get a full census; large districts cap at **12 schools/band**, picked via seeded random sample with most-constrained-first cross-band overlap minimization. This is a fixed *queue-time* commitment — Stage 1 has no extracted minutes yet to judge "is the mode stable," so it must hand Stage 7/8 the full upper-bound candidate list (up to 12/band) rather than try to early-exit itself.

**Decision 2 — extraction-time mode-stability early-exit (still open, blocked on per-school extract→aggregate):** *within* the queued candidates, does extraction need to process all 12, or can it stop once the modal gross-minutes is stable (e.g., unchanged over the last ~5 schools processed)? LA elementary would resolve in ~8 schools, not all 12 queued — but this can only be measured once Stage 7's per-school extract→aggregate is built and run on real captured inputs (Open decision #2 below). The 95/±5 number stands only as the conservative *upper bound* this whole policy replaced.

### Reader-modality head-to-head (2026-06-21) — THREE input classes, format-route the reader
Two tests. **CORRECTION:** an initial test wrongly concluded "`page.pdf()` is useless" — but that test was **biased by construction** (it sampled only pages with innerText≈0, i.e. already-image-based pages, so of course PDF inherited the emptiness). A corrected test on **text-RICH** pages overturns it. Net finding: there are **three input classes**, each with a different best reader:

1. **Clean single-column text** (e.g. Pueblo HS) — `innerText` and `pdftotext -layout` tie; cheap text reader is fine.
2. **CSS multi-column schedules** (e.g. Washoe McQueen — three schedule variants side-by-side) — **`innerText` FLATTENS the columns into a vertical list (loses which column a row belongs to), but Playwright `page.pdf()` + `pdftotext -layout` PRESERVES the column alignment.** This is a real, cheap (no-vision) win — validates the "Save as PDF keeps structure" intuition. *(NB: the right PDF reader here is `pdftotext -layout`, NOT `pdfplumber` — these are CSS-laid-out pages with no ruling-line `<table>` borders, so pdfplumber pulls nav chrome, not the schedule. Caveat: print-CSS can reflow/clip on some hub pages and HURT — so route, don't blanket-apply.)*
3. **Image/canvas-rendered schedules** (e.g. Mat-Su whs/pjm — innerText, pdftotext, pdfplumber, AND screenshot-OCR all 0) — **only VISION reads these** (Gemini Flash got `Wasilla HS 08:45–14:15`, `Palmer Jr 08:00–14:30`). 107 zero-innerText candidates exist in the discovery set; vision isn't competing with text here, it's the only reader that works.

**Implications:** (a) **DO capture `page.pdf()`** (Chromium print path ≈ Chrome "Save as PDF") in addition to innerText + screenshot — it cheaply rescues CSS-multi-column structure that innerText destroys, keeping those pages on the *cheap text council* instead of escalating to vision. (b) **Vision is a first-class reader for the image class** (escalate when text/pdftotext return empty/garbled — cost-gated per the user's logic). (c) The "vision underperformed" bake-off was on clean-text inputs and does NOT apply to the image class. (d) This is **format-routing** (cheap text → pdftotext-layout-on-PDF → vision), not a blanket switch. *(All from 2-3 page spikes — directional; confirm at scale against the new GT before wiring.)*

#### Reader-routing spec (formalized 2026-06-21) — try the cheapest reader that works, escalate only on failure
The router is **outcome-based**, not format-based: it runs the cheap reader, checks whether the output is *usable*, and escalates only when it isn't. (Format only *hints* the starting tier; the success check decides escalation. Tiers are fallbacks, not parallel — vision is never paid for if text already worked.)

```
TIER 1 — plain TEXT (cheapest):  send TEXT to the cheap council
  WHEN innerText OR pdftotext yields usable schedule content
       (≥1 plausible start/end time, or an explicit "starts at hh:mm … ends at hh:mm" phrase)
  COVERS single-column period schedules; paragraph prose

TIER 2 — PDF with structure (still cheap, no vision):  send the PDF (read via `pdftotext -layout`) to the cheap council
  WHEN text is present but its STRUCTURE was lost (multi-column flattened) AND
       page.pdf()/source-PDF + `pdftotext -layout` recovers the column alignment
  COVERS CSS multi-column tables; side-by-side schedule variants
  (reader = pdftotext -layout, NOT pdfplumber, for CSS-laid-out pages)

TIER 2.5 — OCR an image (cheap, between text and vision):  OCR the image, send recovered TEXT to the cheap council
  WHEN the source is a raster image (.png/.jpg/.webp/.gif — NO text layer) but OCR recovers usable content
  COVERS clean image fliers (e.g. Cleveland .webp: tesseract got 176 times, 93 schools cleanly)

TIER 3 — VISION (most expensive, last resort):  send the SCREENSHOT/image to the vision council
  WHEN OCR also fails or scrambles (styled/low-contrast text, e.g. Mat-Su; or multi-column scans, e.g. New Haven CT)
  COVERS styled-graphic text OCR can't read; multi-column scans OCR scrambles
```
*(Note: a raster image — `.png`/`.jpg`/`.webp`/`.gif` — has **no text layer** (unlike a PDF), so it always needs OCR or vision. But "image" is NOT monolithic: a **clean image flier OCRs fine and cheaply** (Cleveland .webp), while **styled/low-contrast** images (Mat-Su) or **multi-column scans** (New Haven CT) defeat OCR → vision. So the Tier-3 trigger is "**did OCR recover usable content**," not "is it an image.")

**Escalation gates:**
- **Tier 2→3 (well-defined):** "no usable content" = count plausible times in all text outputs; below threshold → vision.
- **Tier 1→2 (OPEN — not yet a buildable trigger):** detecting *"text present but structure lost"* is the hard, unsolved part. Washoe's page had 122 innerText times yet the columns were flattened/ambiguous — so the trigger is **not** "few times." Candidate signals (undecided): low band-coverage despite many times; inability to bind times→bands; or the council itself returning low confidence. **Left open deliberately — define before wiring Tier 2.**

#### Broward (2026-06-21) — multi-PAGE column-snake is a SEPARATE failure axis from multi-column
Broward (1200180, a `hub` district) captured **elementary (111 schools, gross 360) and middle (10, 400) accurately — but ZERO high schools** (proposal has no high band). Diagnosis (user-observed): the schedule is a **multi-page, multi-column continuous flow** — Page1 cols1&2 → cols3&4 → Page2 cols1&2 → … — with the elem/middle/high section breaks living *inside* that snaking stream, not as clean per-band blocks. When `pdftotext -layout` / innerText linearizes a multi-*page* multi-*column* doc, reading order tangles across page boundaries, and the **tail of the stream (high schools) degrades most** → high dropped while elementary (front of stream) came through clean. **CONFIRMED in source:** the 3-page PDF has 132 "elementary", 37 "middle", 28 "high" mentions with clean times — high schools ARE present (`Anderson Boyd H. High 7:40–2:40`, `Coconut Creek High 6:50–1:35`), so they were **dropped from a present, readable signal**, not absent. The layout puts **two schools side-by-side per row** (e.g. `Tropical Elementary 8:25–2:25 │ Anderson Boyd H. High 7:40–2:40`) — elementary and high interleaved across column-pairs; extraction captured the left pair (elementary) and lost most of the right pair (middle/high).
- **Two distinct axes:** PDF capture **solved multi-COLUMN** (the captured schools were accurate — strong evidence for `page.pdf()`). Multi-**PAGE continuous flow** is a *separate, unsolved* axis; PDF/pdftotext handles columns-on-one-page but not columns-snaking-across-pages.
- **Strong unused signal — band is encoded in the school NAME** (Broward names schools "… High School" / "… Middle"). Extraction relied on positional grouping and missed this. **FIX CANDIDATE: use band-from-school-name as a disambiguation/recovery signal** in extraction+aggregation (we already have `school_sampling.bands_for` for NCES rosters; the *council extraction* should also lean on the name signal, not just page structure).
- **Topology routing implication (reinforces REQ-057):** per-school queueing **sidesteps** this for `per_school` districts (each HS discovered/captured individually). But Broward is `hub` — per-school queueing can't save it; the hub path needs the **name signal and/or layout-aware reading** (vision handles multi-page layout natively). So hub-vs-per_school routing sends these down different solution paths.

#### Orange (2026-06-21) — INPUT TRUNCATION (`MAX_TEXT_LEN`), a different bug than Broward
Orange (1201440, `hub`) lost the second half of its middle schools (all → unresolved) and **all page-4 high schools**. Root cause is **our own input cap, not the models and not an API/context limit**: `gt_propose`/`extractors` truncate the document to `MAX_TEXT_LEN = 12000` chars (`txt[:MAX_TEXT_LEN]`). Orange's source is **16,618 chars**; the consensus "fell apart at Howard Middle" because **"Howard" sits at char 11,890 — right at the 12,000 cutoff.** Everything after (rest of middle + all high) was **never sent to the models** — not mis-read, *unsent*.
- **Distinct from Broward:** Broward's high schools were *inside* the 12K window but lost to column-snaking; Orange's were *cut off entirely* by the char cap. Different fixes.
- **FIX (deterministic, cheap): chunk large inputs, don't truncate.** Since we aggregate per-school anyway, feed a multi-page hub in page/section chunks and union the per-school facts — loses nothing, removes the silent tail-drop. (Raising `MAX_TEXT_LEN` is a stopgap; chunking is the real fix and also helps Broward by keeping per-page column structure local.) **`MAX_TEXT_LEN` truncation must not silently drop schedule content — any source exceeding the cap must be chunked + aggregated, never sliced.**

### Deferred hard input cases (come back to — expensive, low priority for now)
These source formats are *known to contain schedules* but are costly to extract reliably; explicitly out of scope for the current build, recorded so they aren't forgotten:
- **Bell schedules buried in Parent/Student Handbooks** — long multi-topic PDFs where the schedule is a few lines among dozens of pages. Needs locating the right page/section before extraction (a retrieval step our current "the captured page IS the schedule" assumption skips). Several manually-collected sources turned out to be this.
- **Expanding/accordion hub pages** (e.g. Anchorage `School Start List` — collapsible sections that only render fully on interaction). The tiered capture's screenshot misses collapsed content; would need scripted expansion before capture. Excluded from the curated GT for this reason.
- **Image-only schedules requiring layout reasoning** (rotated tables, multi-column posters) beyond what OCR linearizes cleanly.
General principle: these are **retrieval/rendering** problems upstream of extraction, not model-quality problems — defer until the core per-school path is proven on clean inputs.

- **Multi-column layouts that OCR linearizes wrong (NOTE — confidence signal, not solved).** A page with side-by-side school|times column *pairs* (e.g. New Haven CT 0902790: cols 1&2 = school/time, cols 3&4 = school/time) is read by `tesseract` as separated *runs* — all names lumped together, times elsewhere — destroying the row→school binding. Models then *guess* the school↔time pairing from the scrambled stream and **make the SAME mis-pairing** (Col-3 names paired with Col-2 times; Col-1 names dropped entirely). Critically, this produces a **FALSE consensus**: the council agrees not because each model independently read it right, but because the shared bad input misled them identically — and "confirmed" values can be right only by coincidence (e.g. Col-2 times happening to equal Col-4 times where the district uses uniform hours). **Implication: cross-model agreement on a known multi-column/garbled-OCR source is weak evidence — it must DOWN-weight confidence, not auto-accept** (when reading OCR *text*).
**RESOLVED by vision (spike 2026-06-21):** sending the *rendered page PNG* (not OCR text) to **Gemini 2.5 Flash + Mistral Large 2512 in vision mode** read New Haven CT's 4-column layout **spatially correct** — recovered the Column-1 names OCR-text dropped, paired each school with its own times, and the two cross-family models *agreed on the correct reads* (real consensus, since the input was no longer scrambled). Flash 59 / Mistral 56 schedules vs the broken OCR-text result. **Takeaway: for multi-column / scanned sources, vision-read the rendered PNG — it's a working fix we already have (the harness `extract(images=[...])` + OpenRouter `image_url` path), not deferred research.** Vision defeats the shared-bad-input false-consensus because a legible image is no longer a shared *bad* input. (Caveat: vision underperformed in the earlier text-vs-vision bake-off on *clean* inputs — so use vision as a **format-routed reader for image/scan/multi-column sources**, not a blanket replacement for clean digital text.)

**Now handled (2026-06-21): scanned / image-only PDFs (no text layer).** A PDF that is a printed-then-scanned page (JBIG2/CCITT raster, empty `pdffonts`, `pdftotext`→~0 chars) cannot be read by `pdftotext` *or* by `tesseract` directly (tesseract needs a raster image, not a PDF). Fix: the OCR fallback **rasterizes each PDF page to PNG via `pdftoppm -r 200` first, then OCRs** (`gt_propose._ocr_pdf`). Recovered New Haven CT (0902790) from `no_readable_times` to a full 3-band per-school list (125 time patterns). Note: OCR of scans is noisier → more `unresolved` schools from garbled school names (matching misses), but resolved schools still drive solid band values. The same rasterize-then-OCR step must exist anywhere the pipeline OCRs PDFs.

## Human-QC strategy (the binding constraint)

Two independent extractors disagree on a large share of districts; at <1 hr/week, human review can't drain a 20K queue. Resolution: **decouple coverage from verification** — auto-accept on council agreement, statutory-fallback the uncertain tail (labeled), and **spend human QC enrollment-weighted** (the top few hundred districts dominate every published LCT number; ~500 × 2 min ≈ feasible). See `INSTRUCTIONAL_MINUTES_ACQUISITION_STRATEGY.md` for the full constraint analysis.
